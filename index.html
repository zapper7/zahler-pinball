The Story

A dad, a kid, and three straight World Championships

A dad lifts the glass off a pinball machine and starts placing the ball by hand for his kid. Years later, that kid is the #1-ranked player on Earth with three consecutive World Championships. Players keep asking us how. So we're finally writing it all down.

Jason Zahler

World #1 · 3× World Champion

Playing since age two and a half, competing since childhood, studying nutrition and exercise science in college, and now the only player to threepeat the IFPA World Championship. As of July 2026 he's also the only player ever to win the Pinburgh World Match-Play Championship three years in a row. He'll show you the saves that look like magic, and prove they aren't. Still hasn't beaten his dad at PAC-MAN (some records take longer to threepeat).

Steve Zahler

Head Coach · The Dad

Taekwondo guy, owner of Modern Pinball NYC ("Pinball is back!"), where he taught lessons and ran classes for school groups. Glass-off drills, warm-ups, sleep schedules: he coached Jason like a taekwondo drill sergeant, and now puts the same regimen in your inbox. Once did seven minutes on FOX's Good Day New York, then caught a flight to Germany for IFPA10.

AGE DOESN'T MATTER

It cuts both ways. Jason played his first tournament at age 7 and won his first at age 9, against players multiple times his age. (That same year, he and his sister Maxine were featured on NBC's TODAY Show with Lester Holt.)

Meanwhile, Steve started competitive pinball at 41 and achieved his highest world ranking 14 years later, beating players half, even a quarter, his age. Whatever your age, you're not too late. You're right on time.

What lands in your inbox

Things you'll wish someone had told you years ago

Not vague advice. The specific stuff (the moves, the routines, the tournament habits) that took Jason and Steve thousands of games to figure out so you don't have to.

  • SAVE №1 The save that looks like magic Most balls headed straight down the middle only look that way. Learn to see the lean, and what your hands should already be doing.
  • VISION Read the ball before it reaches your flippers Knowing the future has its rewards. Rubber types, bounce angles, and where the ball is going next.
  • BODY The Perfect Pinball Warm-up Taekwondo-derived, takes minutes, needs no equipment. Steve and Jason do it before every tournament day.
  • BRAIN The sleep pattern that peaks you at finals Most of this game is mental. Sleep, caffeine, hydration, and focus, timed so you're sharpest when it counts.
  • STRATEGY How Jason qualifies #1, again and again Entry management, game selection, and "which games to avoid." (A lot of it is situational. He'll explain that too.)
  • NERVE Playing calm when everything is loud Breathing, tilt discipline, handling pressure. Play calm and carry on.
  • NEW ON THE FLOOR Reviews of every new machine, from every manufacturer Rules breakdowns and early strategy before you waste your first game guessing. Stern Pinball, Jersey Jack Pinball, Chicago Gaming, American Pinball, Haggis: all of it.

Fair questions

Is it actually free?

Yes. Signing up is free, and every new edition lands in your inbox free. Some day there may be paid extras: courses, coaching, an emergency pinball hotline (really). The tips keep showing up either way.

I'm a beginner. Too early?

No. The fundamentals are where champions are made. Steve coached Jason from age two and a half. If you can reach the flippers, you're ready.

I'm already ranked. Too late?

Also no. Tournament craft, qualification strategy, energy management: this is the layer above flipper skills. It's the part most ranked players never train.
