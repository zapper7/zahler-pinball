Look before you plunge.

How to play any pinball machine you walk up to: the 30-second habit that separates players from passengers. From the coach's side of the glass.

Coach Dad speaking.

Tip #1

Jason will write plenty of these. The first one's mine, because the first thing I ever taught him wasn't a flipper skill. It was a habit, and it's the same habit I'd drill into you if you were standing next to me: look at the machine before you touch it.

Most players walk up, hit start, and plunge. They start learning the machine during ball one, which is a very expensive classroom. Meanwhile the player who spent thirty seconds looking first is already ahead before the ball moves. In a tournament, that gap is the match.

The scan, in order:

  1. The flippers. Watch someone else play if you can, or note it fast on your first trap: strong or mushy? A weak flipper changes which shots are even worth attempting.
  2. The slope and the outlanes. Steep machines play fast and drain mean. Wide-open outlanes tell you to favor control over risk all game.
  3. The score display (DMD, LCD, etc.). What's lit, what's the machine begging you to shoot? Modern Pinball games (😉) tell you the strategy right on the display. Almost nobody reads it.
  4. Other players' drains. The machine is showing you exactly where it kills people. Free intelligence. Take it.

None of this requires Jason's hands. It's discipline, thirty seconds of it, and it's the difference between playing the machine and being a passenger on it. I taught it to a kid who couldn't see over the glass. It scaled up fine: three world championships in a row, plus a Pinburgh threepeat for good measure. He took it places I'm trying to follow.
