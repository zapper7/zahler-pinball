Look before you plunge.

How to play any pinball machine you walk up to: the 30-second habit that separates the pros from the casual players. From the coach's side of the glass.

Coach Dad speaking.

Tip #1

Jason will write plenty of these. The first one’s mine, because the first thing I ever taught him about pinball wasn’t a flipper skill. It was a habit, and it’s the same habit I’d teach you if you were standing next to me:

Look at the machine before you touch it.

Most players walk up, hit start and plunge. They begin learning the machine during ball one, when every mistake counts. Meanwhile, the player who spent 30 seconds looking first already has useful information before the ball moves.

In a tournament, that small advantage can make the difference.

The scan, in order:

The flippers. Watch someone else play if you can. If you can’t, make a note of it quickly on your first trap: strong or weak? A weak flipper changes which shots are worth attempting and how you should approach them.

The slope and the outlanes. Watch how quickly the ball moves and returns to the flippers. A steep machine usually plays faster and gives you less time to react. Wide-open outlanes tell you to favor control over risk.

The display (DMD, LCD, etc.). What’s lit? What is the machine indicating that you should shoot? Modern pinball games provide a surprising amount of useful information on the display. Many players don’t pay enough attention to it.

Other players’ drains. Notice where and how they happen. If balls keep draining from the same shot or area of the playfield, the machine is telling you what to be careful of. Use that information.

None of this requires Jason’s hands. It requires observation and about 30 seconds of discipline. It’s the difference between playing the machine with a plan and simply reacting to whatever happens.

I taught this to a kid who couldn’t see over the glass. He still does it before every game, including on his way to three consecutive World Championships and a Pinburgh threepeat.

His game changed quite a bit over the years. That habit never did.

A note from Jason
